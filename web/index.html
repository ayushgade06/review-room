T20 cricket · Decision Review System · win-probability economics

How sure does a captain need to be before reviewing?

Every review risks something small to win something big. We priced both sides of that bet, ball by ball, for every recorded review in men's T20 cricket.

    06

    Method & limits

    The decision

    Review when p · G > (1 − p) · (1 − u) · L, so the break-even belief is p* = (1 − u)L / (G + (1 − u)L).

    G = win-probability swing of the wicket in question. L = option value lost if the review fails outright. u = share of failed reviews that end as umpire's call (the review is retained).

    The models

    Win probability: gradient-boosted trees with monotonic constraints on par-relative features (runs against the competition's recent scoring), one per innings. Option value: mean future win probability gained from overturns by teams still holding k reviews at that over, isotonic-smoothed. Allowances (1 or 2 unsuccessful reviews per innings) are inferred from the review sequences and match the published IPL rule change in 2022.

    What this does not claim

    We never see appeals that were not reviewed, or ball-tracking. A captain's belief p is theirs to supply; the tool supplies the threshold. Option values reflect current reviewing behaviour and DRS-recorded seasons only. Win probabilities are model estimates, not certainties; associations are not causal effects.
