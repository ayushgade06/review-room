Cricket's version of the NFL challenge flag · 5,132 real challenges analysed

When should a captain throw the challenge flag?

In cricket, a team can challenge an umpire's call with video replay — just like an NFL coach's red flag or an MLB manager's challenge. We worked out how sure a team needs to be before challenging, using every recorded challenge in men's T20 cricket.

    00

    New to cricket? Start here

    You only need four ideas to follow everything on this page.

    1. A wicket is an out. Each team bats once, for up to 120 balls (a T20 match), and is done after 10 outs. Losing a batter is a big deal — like an out with the bases loaded.
    2. The umpire makes a call. On the spot, the umpire rules a batter "out" or "not out". The most disputed calls are LBW (the ball hit the batter's leg when it would have hit the stumps) and thin catches.
    3. Either team can challenge it — that's DRS. The Decision Review System is cricket's replay challenge: the same idea as the NFL red flag, MLB's manager challenge or a tennis Hawk-Eye challenge. Video and ball-tracking decide.
    4. Challenges are limited. A team can lose two challenges per innings in most leagues. If the replay is too close to call ("umpire's call"), the original decision stands but the team keeps its challenge.

    So every challenge is a small bet: risk a challenge you might need later, to fix a call that might cost you the game. This project measures both sides of that bet in "win chance" — the percentage chance each team wins, updated after every ball, like the win-probability line on a TV broadcast.

    Method & limits

    The bet, in one line

    Challenge if your confidence × what the call is worth is bigger than your doubt × what a lost challenge costs.

    Rearranged, the minimum confidence is p* = (1 − u)L / (G + (1 − u)L), where G is what the call is worth, L what a failed challenge costs, and u the share of failures that are "umpire's call" (challenge kept).

    How the pieces are measured

    What a call is worth: the drop in win chance from losing that batter, from a model trained on 8,897 matches — think of a TV win-probability line. What a failed challenge costs: how much win chance teams typically gain later from the challenges they still hold, measured from real matches. Challenges left: rebuilt from the order of challenges in each innings; it matches the IPL's 2022 rule change from one to two.

    What this does not claim

    We cannot see calls nobody challenged, and we have no ball-tracking data, so the player's own read of the ball is the missing ingredient — the tool asks for it rather than guessing it. All numbers are estimates, and patterns in behaviour are not proof of cause and effect.
